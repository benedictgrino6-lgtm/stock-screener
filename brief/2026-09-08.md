Two flags today. Neither is strong, and both have a plain explanation. Nothing here needs
action — this is a "keep half an eye on it" day.

## AMD — big call money, but on a big up day

**What's important:** AMD closed up 6.7%, and $207M of call premium traded against just $15M
of puts. The lopsidedness matters more than it looks: measured by dollars it's a 14-to-1
call skew, versus only 2-to-1 by contract count — meaning the call buying was in large,
expensive contracts, not cheap far-out lottery tickets. Implied volatility on calls also
rose above puts, which is unusual and points the same way.

**The opportunity:** the question is whether this is real positioning or just people
chasing a 6.7% move on the day. The tell is open interest — it barely moved at the active
strikes (a few hundred to ~1,200 new contracts against volumes of 8,000–20,000), which
leans toward "day-trading the move." If tomorrow the premium *and* open interest keep
building after the move-day churn clears, it starts to look like someone accumulating ahead
of something. That's the thing to check.

**The catch:** the 6.7% rally explains almost all of this on its own, and you can't tell
whether the calls were bought fresh or sold against stock. Most likely: nothing.

## AAPL — a strange put, second day running

**What's important:** for two sessions now, a put 26% below AAPL's price expiring the next
day has drawn heavy volume — 14,600 new contracts of open interest. On its own that's a
bet on a >25% one-day crash, which nobody makes. It almost certainly isn't a standalone
position.

**The opportunity:** thin. The realistic read is that it's one leg of a spread (there'd be
a matching trade at a nearer strike) or a cheap crash hedge on a large stock holding. Worth
30 seconds to check for the other leg. If it shows up a *third* day, that's more
interesting and worth a proper look at AAPL news.

**The catch:** it's most likely a hedge or a spread leg — structural, not directional.
